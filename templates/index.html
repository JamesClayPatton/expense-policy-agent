How it works

Expense request The form above: employee, amount, category, vendor, approval on record, notes, receipt text. The notes and receipt are the claimant's words and are treated as claims, not facts.
1. Retrieve policy (RAG) The policy is stored one row per section in pgvector. Eight control sections (eligibility, approval authority, splits, duplicates, budgets, prohibited items, unaddressed cases) are always in context; similarity search adds the four closest category sections.
2. Agent (gpt-5-mini in a LangGraph loop) Must call a tool every turn: look up the employee, the remaining monthly budget, or earlier claims at the same vendor, or call Decision to finish.
3. Decision Approve, flag for a human, or reject, with the policy sections relied on and a two-sentence reason.
4. Guard (plain code, not a model) Looks the facts up itself and can only make the decision stricter. An approve becomes a flag on a missing or made-up citation, an inactive employee, missing or over-limit approval, self-approval, a duplicate on file, an overspent budget, or a late submission.
5. Record The one write in the system, always last: the decision, what the agent saw, and the link to its LangSmith trace. The dashboard reads from here.
6. Judge (claude-sonnet-5-5) A different model scores the reason 1 to 5 on grounded, complete, clear and safe, with a quoted piece of evidence for each.
